# Affiliate Strategy — Software Engineer + Musician

> [!summary]
> A personalised affiliate plan for someone who (a) can only **produce** content in a weekend-heavy schedule, and (b) has deep credibility in **software engineering** and **playing a musical instrument**. The headline conclusion: build a two-pillar **authority** site around dev/SaaS tools and music-tech, monetise primarily with **recurring [[Revenue Share]]** (SaaS) and **[[Cost per Sale]]** (music software + gear), and exploit your rare SE×musician overlap as the wedge.

---

## 1. Your constraints — and why one of them barely matters

### Time budget

| When | Window | Block length | Energy (after a SE day job) |
|------|--------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Mon–Fri | 22:00 – 23:59 | ~2 h, short | **Low** — tired, late |
| Sat–Sun | 09:00 – 23:59 | up to ~15 h, long | **High** — fresh, uninterrupted |

Weekly ceiling ≈ **40 h**, but it is *lumpy*: ~10 h of short, low-energy weeknight slots + ~30 h of long weekend blocks. Plan for realism — nobody sustains 15 productive hours/day; treat weekends as **2 deep 4–6 h sessions**, not 30 h.

### The reframing that changes everything

> [!important] Affiliate income is **asynchronous**. Your schedule limits *production*, not *earning*.
> A review you publish once earns commission whenever a reader converts — 3 AM your time, any day, while you sleep or work your day job. So:
> - **Time zone vs. audience does NOT matter** for SEO/content income. Your buyers are mostly US/EU; your VN night-time production never touches their buying time, and that's fine.
> - What your schedule *does* rule out: anything **synchronous or velocity-dependent** — daily deal/coupon posting, live-stream-as-primary, news-jacking, real-time social cadence, fast lead-response offers.

This single fact points you at **evergreen, batch-produced, search-driven content** and away from anything that needs you online at a particular moment.

---

## 2. Schedule → task mapping

Match task type to block type. Don't waste a long fresh weekend block on email, and don't attempt deep writing at 11 PM Tuesday.

| Slot | Best for |
|------|----------|
| **Weeknight (2 h, tired)** | Keyword research, outlining, replying to comments, link/analytics checks, small edits, scheduling, admin, learning. Low-stakes, interruptible. |
| **Weekend (long, fresh)** | The real work: writing long-form reviews/comparisons, **recording instrument demos & video**, **building tools/micro-sites** (your SE superpower), batch-producing and scheduling a week of content. |

> [!tip] Batch on weekends, drip on weeknights.
> Produce 1–3 substantial assets per weekend; use weeknights to promote, maintain, and prep the next batch. This rhythm fits your lumpy time better than trying to do a little every day.

---

## 3. Product categories that fit *you*

Pick the intersection of **(your expertise → trust → conversion)** and **(good affiliate economics)**. You have two strong domains and a rare overlap.

### Pillar A — Software / SaaS / Dev tools  ⭐ primary

Why it fits: you can evaluate these credibly, the buyers are global and search-driven, and the economics are the best in affiliate marketing — **recurring commissions** and high [[Lifetime Value|LTV]].

- Cloud hosting & infra (managed hosting, VPS, serverless, CDNs)
- Developer SaaS (IDEs, monitoring/observability, CI/CD, API tools, DBs, auth)
- No-code / automation (workflow tools, site builders)
- Programming courses & learning platforms
- Dev-adjacent hardware (mechanical keyboards, monitors, docks) — [[Cost per Sale|CPS]], high [[Average Order Value|AOV]]

> [!tip] Your unmatched moat: you can **build**, not just write.
> Most affiliates can only publish prose. As a SE you can ship **free micro-tools** — calculators, comparison engines, config generators, CLI utilities, browser extensions — that rank in search, earn backlinks, and funnel to affiliate offers. A single useful tool can out-earn fifty articles and compounds while you sleep. This is the highest-leverage use of your weekend blocks.

### Pillar B — Music tech for musicians  ⭐ secondary

Why it fits: you *play*, so you can demo gear and software with authority no spec-sheet blogger can fake. Audio/video demos build trust fast.

- **Music software** (VST plugins, DAWs, sample libraries, presets) — digital, higher margins, demoable. → [[Cost per Sale|CPS]], sometimes recurring.
- **Online music courses / lesson subscriptions** — subscription [[Lifetime Value|LTV]], sometimes [[Revenue Share|recurring]].
- **Instruments & studio gear** (audio interfaces, mics, MIDI controllers, monitors) — high [[Average Order Value|AOV]] but low % and short cookies (see §5). Treat as a **supplement**, not the core.

### The wedge — where A meets B  💡

The SE×musician combo is *rare*, and a niche you uniquely own:

- Audio programming / DSP, MIDI tooling, building music apps
- DAW automation & scripting, home-studio setups for developers
- "Music tech for people who code" / "tools for musician-developers"

Less competition, instant authority, and it lets you cross-sell both pillars. **Start here if you want a defensible niche rather than fighting giants in generic "best web hosting."**

## 4. Which payout model — ranked for your situation

Your scarcest resource is *production time*, so favour models where **one asset earns repeatedly** and you need **few conversions** to matter. (Each links to its full note.)

| Rank | Model | Why it fits a time-constrained SE+musician | Use it for |
|------|-------|--------------------------------------------|------------|
| 1 | **[[Revenue Share]]** (recurring) | Build once → compound monthly as subscribers renew. Best ROI on scarce time; rewards authority over volume. | SaaS, dev tools, hosting, lesson subscriptions |
| 2 | **[[Cost per Sale]]** (CPS) | Digital music software = high margin; gear = high [[Average Order Value|AOV]] so few sales pay well. | Music software/plugins, courses, instruments, dev hardware |
| 3 | **[[Cost per Action]]** hybrid / **[[Cost per Registration|CPR]]** | Many SaaS pay a signup bounty *plus* recurring — take both. CPR works for freemium dev-tool sign-ups. | Freemium SaaS, dev tools with free tiers |
| ✗ | **[[Cost per Lead]] / [[Cost per Quality Lead]]** | Off-brand for you, fraud-sensitive, needs **volume + fast response** your schedule can't supply. Finance/insurance/iGaming lead-gen ignores your expertise. | **Skip** unless you abandon the authority play |

> [!tip] Compare every offer on [[Earnings per Click]] (EPC), not headline rate.
> A 5%-on-$900 music-interface sale beats 10%-on-$40, and a 30% *recurring* SaaS commission with a 90-day [[Cookie Duration|cookie]] beats a one-off bounty once the customer stays past break-even (see [[Lifetime Value]]). EPC folds rate × conversion × value into one comparable number.

Benchmark: good SaaS programs pay **20–40%**, cookies **30–120 days**, recurring on the subscriber's billing cycle.

### Pillar B — Music

| Program | Reported terms | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Plugin Boutique | Professional affiliate program | VSTs, DAWs, sample libs, **music courses** — your demo-able sweet spot |

## 6. Risks & honest caveats

> [!warning] Things that will bite if ignored
> - **Time-to-traffic is long.** SEO content typically takes **6–12 months** to rank. This is not fast money; it's an asset you compound. Your weekend-batch model suits the long game — just don't expect Q1 income.
> - **Short cookies undercut gear income.** Thomann's 14-day and Amazon's 24-h [[Cookie Duration|cookies]] silently kill credited sales. Prefer 30–90-day programs.
> - **Returns & churn claw back earnings.** Gear returns trigger [[Reversal|reversals]]; SaaS churn erodes [[Revenue Share]]. Promote sticky products; track *confirmed*, not pending, earnings.
> - **FTC/disclosure is mandatory.** Every page with affiliate links needs a clear disclosure. As an engineer, template it once.
> - **Don't spread thin.** Two pillars is already ambitious for ~40 lumpy hours/week. Consider launching **Pillar A or the wedge first**, add Pillar B once the rhythm holds.

## 7. 90-day action plan (mapped to your schedule)

> [!example]- Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4: Foundation
> - **Weeknights:** pick the niche (recommend the SE×musician **wedge** or Pillar A), keyword research, apply to 3–5 programs from §5 (verify terms), draft a disclosure template.
> - **Weekends:** stand up the site (you're an SE — a static site + analytics is a weekend), publish 2 cornerstone reviews/comparisons, start **one free micro-tool**.

> [!example]- Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8: Production rhythm
> - **Weeknights:** outline next assets, reply to any comments, monitor analytics & [[Earnings per Click|EPC]].
> - **Weekends:** ship 1–2 long-form assets each weekend (one written, one with an **instrument/tool demo video**); finish and launch the micro-tool; internal-link everything.

> [!example]- Phase 3 — Weeks 9–12: Compound & measure
> - **Weeknights:** track which offers actually convert; prune dead links; double down on winners.
> - **Weekends:** build the *second* tool or a comparison engine; start an email list (recurring audience asset); evaluate adding Pillar B if Pillar A is humming.

---

## 8. Bottom line

> [!summary] One-paragraph answer
> Build an **authority content site + free tools** in the **SE×musician** space. Monetise core with **recurring [[Revenue Share]]** SaaS/dev programs (compounding income that fits scarce time) and **[[Cost per Sale]]** on music software, courses, and high-[[Average Order Value|AOV]] gear. Use **[[Cost per Registration|CPR]]/hybrid** opportunistically on freemium dev tools. **Skip [[Cost per Lead|CPL]]/[[Cost per Quality Lead|CPQL]]** lead-gen — it's off-brand and needs velocity you don't have. Batch-produce on weekends, maintain on weeknights, and lean on your ability to **build tools** as the moat. Compare offers on [[Earnings per Click|EPC]], prefer long [[Cookie Duration|cookies]] and high [[Lifetime Value|LTV]], and play the 6–12 month long game.
